bettertyping 2 · art direction · round two · futurist

A precision instrument that lives in the future.

You took the weight and the measurement logic of A and the depth and atmosphere of C. All three below are that hybrid — condensed technical labels, tabular readouts and calibration marks, set in volumetric dark rather than on paper.

They differ in where the intelligence shows: E1 shows it as telemetry, E2 hides it behind an optical instrument, E3 puts you inside the model's own view.

E1

Telemetry

Graphite ground, hairline frame, corner registration marks, dense micro-typography. The discipline is absolute: the interface is monochrome and colour belongs exclusively to data. The spectral ramp — magenta through indigo to ice — is your speed, and it appears nowhere except the trace, the caret and the heatmap. Restraint is what makes it read as an instrument rather than a video game.

The Line here

A spectral trace whose hue is your instantaneous speed, with keystroke event marks ticking beneath it. On finish it detaches and the ramp becomes the legend of your results chart.

E2

Aperture

C's chamber with an optical instrument built into it — a focusing ring, hairline crosshairs, one cold light source below the horizon. Words ahead sit out of focus and resolve as you reach them, so the depth of field is doing real work, not decoration. The most serene of the three, and the easiest to look at for an hour.

E3

Machine Vision

The interface is what the analysis engine sees. Words carry detection boxes, the active word sits in a reticle, and the rail forecasts which bigrams are about to cost you — because after a few tests it genuinely can. The trace is drawn inside a confidence band. Nothing here is faked for looks: every number is one we already compute.

The one caveat: annotations live in the ready state, the results and the dashboard. The moment you start typing they fade to just the reticle, or they would wreck the very focus we are protecting.

My recommendation

E1 — Telemetry, taking E3's confidence band and forecast rail into the results and analytics views.

E1 holds the whole product together: it is calm enough to type in every morning, precise enough to look like it knows something, and its one rule — monochrome interface, chromatic data — is a discipline that keeps every future screen on-identity without me having to re-decide. E3's ideas are too good to lose, but they belong where you are reading rather than typing. E2 is the most beautiful of the three and the least differentiated; its atmosphere is a treatment other sites could copy in an afternoon.

Say a number. Or mix them — "E1 but with E2's fog behind it" is exactly the kind of note I want, and costs nothing today.
