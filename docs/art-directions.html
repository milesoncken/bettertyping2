bettertyping 2 · art direction · four explorations

The last one was the genre default wearing a new coat.

Dark ground, one warm accent, monospace everywhere — that is the typing-site house style, and I handed it back to you as an identity. Fair hit.

Here are four directions that could not be mistaken for anything else in the category. Each is a real screen, not a mood board: the same moment of the same test, rendered four ways. Read them as whole worlds — palette, type, layout and the behaviour of the Line all move together.

A

Instrument

A precision measuring device, on paper. Plotter grid, calibration ticks, a pen laying ink as you type. Light, not dark — which alone puts daylight between us and every competitor. And it is the only direction whose visual language is the product's actual claim: this machine measures you to the millisecond.

The Line here

A plotter pen. It has a visible head, it draws with slight ink weight variation, and it never lifts. On finish the paper feeds upward and the trace becomes the chart.

B

Kinetic Field

Swiss poster energy at full saturation. A single overwhelming colour field, type set enormous and hard to the grid, one acid highlight doing all the signalling. The loudest option — it wins attention in a scroll-past and photographs beautifully, which matters when judges see the screenshot before the site.

The Line here

Not a chart — a gesture. One thick acid stroke cutting the whole composition. On finish the field collapses to bone and the stroke stays, becoming the axis your results hang from.

C

Chamber

Dark, but volumetric rather than flat — atmosphere, depth of field, a single cold light source. Words ahead of you recede into fog and resolve as you approach. The calmest and the most cinematic, and the only one where the depth does real work: blur removes the visual noise of text you are not typing yet.

A filament of cold light in the fog. It is the only thing in the scene that emits rather than reflects. On finish the camera pulls back and the filament is revealed to have been your curve all along.

D

Overprint

Risograph print shop: two spot inks, real multiply overprint where they cross, halftone dots, everything a degree or two off register. The most tactile and the most human — it makes a keyboard site feel handmade. No typing site has ever looked like this, which is both the argument for it and the risk.

My recommendation

A — Instrument, with the Chamber's depth borrowed for the results reveal and the analytics dashboard.

It is the only direction where the aesthetic argues the product's case. Everything else in the category is a dark screen with coloured letters; a site that looks like a calibrated measuring instrument says we know things about your hands that they don't before a single word is read. Light-first is the differentiator no competitor can copy without abandoning their own identity — and the paper world gives the Line somewhere real to live.

If you want the loudest possible swing, take B. It is the most likely to stop a judge mid-scroll and the most likely to divide people. I would happily build it — it just asks you to accept that some visitors will find it exhausting to use daily, which for a site people open every morning is a genuine cost.

Tell me which letter, or tell me what's wrong with all four — "none of these, but C is closest and I want it warmer" is a completely useful answer. Nothing is built yet, so this is the cheapest possible moment to be difficult.
